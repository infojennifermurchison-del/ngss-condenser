Why Category 2 and not the others

Look at what it costs to move each category. Category 1 means hiring or credentialing staff. Category 4 means furniture, materials, and facility work. Both require capital, and if you had capital sitting there you would have spent it already.

Category 2 is teacher and child interactions. It costs coaching. It is the heaviest weighted category in the rubric and the only one you can move with people you already employ, in classrooms you already have, starting the week after this session.

You already have a mentor. Here is why you are paying me.

Your board assigns you a mentor at no cost and offers free professional development, and you should use both.

What a mentor does is tell you where you are not meeting the standard. What no one teaches you is how to walk into your own classroom on Tuesday, watch a teacher for fifteen minutes, name the one behavior that matters most, and run a conversation that changes what she does on Wednesday. That is a different skill and nobody hands it to you.

This session teaches that skill and gives you the tools to keep running it after the day ends.

What you leave with

A written, dated coaching cycle for the next quarter, built on your own classrooms, ready to run the next business day. Plus the working packet: the Category 2 rubric reference, observation capture sheets, the Coaching Ladder, a coaching conversation template with a worked example, and the cycle planning template.

You will be able to walk into any room in your center, identify the single highest leverage behavior to grow, and run the conversation that grows it.

The honest timeline

This is not a rate increase in October. The chain is: you coach the interactions, your Category 2 score moves at your next assessment, your star level follows, and your rate follows that. Certification work can run up to 24 months from entry level, so where you land depends on where you already sit in your assessment cycle.

Come knowing that. What this session buys you is the ability to start moving the score now instead of after the next assessment tells you what you already suspected.
