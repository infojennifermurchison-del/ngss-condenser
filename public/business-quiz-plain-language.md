# Business Growth Stage Quiz (Plain-Language Version)

Every business goes through a few predictable stages as it grows. This quiz helps you figure out which stage your business is in right now, and gives you a short, focused to-do list for the next six months.

There are five stages: **Just Starting Out, Staying Afloat, Success (Steady or Growing), Growing Fast,** and **Large & Established**.

> 💡 Want the interactive version? Open **`business-quiz.html`** in a browser — it adds up your answers and shows your results automatically.

## How to take it

Answer each question with a simple **Yes** or **No**, based on how your business actually works *today* — not how you hope it will work someday. Be honest; there are no wrong answers. When you're done, see which stage got the most "Yes" answers. That's your stage.

---

## Part 1: Answer the questions

For each question, the more "Yes" answers a stage gets, the more likely that's the stage you're in.

### Stage 1 — Just Starting Out

1. Right now, is most of your energy going into finding customers and actually delivering your product or service? (Yes / No)
2. Are you (the owner) doing almost everything yourself — sales, the actual work, and the paperwork? (Yes / No)
3. Would the business struggle to keep going if you stepped away, or if cash got tight for even a short time? (Yes / No)
4. Do you mostly keep things in your head, without written systems or formal plans? (Yes / No)

### Stage 2 — Staying Afloat

1. Does the business usually bring in enough to cover its bills, but profit is still small or up-and-down? (Yes / No)
2. Is your biggest worry making sure there's enough cash coming in to keep the doors open? (Yes / No)
3. Have you handed off a few tasks to others, but you're still very hands-on day to day? (Yes / No)
4. Do you have a few basic routines in place, but not much long-term planning yet? (Yes / No)

### Stage 3 — Success

This stage comes in two flavors. Answer both sets — whichever gets more "Yes" answers is the one that fits you.

**3a. Comfortable & Steady** (you're happy keeping things as they are)

1. Is the business making a steady profit and giving you a comfortable living? (Yes / No)
2. Could it keep running well even if you were a lot less involved day to day? (Yes / No)
3. Do you have solid systems and money controls — like budgets and reliable bookkeeping — in place? (Yes / No)
4. Are you happy keeping things steady, without a strong push to grow much bigger? (Yes / No)

**3b. Ready to Grow** (you want to expand)

1. Is the business profitable and stable, and are you actively looking for ways to expand? (Yes / No)
2. Are you putting profits back into the business to fuel growth? (Yes / No)
3. Do you have capable managers running daily operations so you can focus on the big picture? (Yes / No)
4. Are you using plans and budgets to guide your growth and spending? (Yes / No)

### Stage 4 — Growing Fast

1. Is your revenue or customer base growing quickly right now? (Yes / No)
2. Are you dealing with growing pains — cash stretched thin, needing to hire fast, or things getting more complex? (Yes / No)
3. Have you handed real decision-making authority to a strong management team? (Yes / No)
4. Is a lot of your focus on planning ahead and making operations more professional to keep up with growth? (Yes / No)

### Stage 5 — Large & Established

1. Is your business large and well-established, with strong finances and plenty of resources? (Yes / No)
2. Are your management systems, planning, and controls highly developed and built into how the company runs? (Yes / No)
3. Is the company good at innovating and adapting when the market changes? (Yes / No)
4. Is your main challenge keeping growth going and staying nimble so the company doesn't get stuck or stale? (Yes / No)

## Part 3: What to focus on next

### Just Starting Out

**Your main goal:** Get the business on its feet and win a steady flow of paying customers.

- **Find customers.** Put most of your time into sales and marketing. Get clear on who your ideal customer is and where to reach them.
- **Make sure people want it.** Listen closely to early customers and keep improving your product or service so it truly solves their problem.
- **Watch your cash.** Check your cash often. Make sure you can cover this month's bills, and look into small funding options if things get tight.
- **Keep it simple.** Set up just enough basic steps to deliver reliably, and keep your costs and overhead low.

### Staying Afloat

**Your main goal:** Reach steady profit and a cash flow you can rely on.

- **Make it more profitable.** Look hard at your prices and costs. Find places to raise your margins or trim spending that isn't pulling its weight.
- **Steady the cash.** Start forecasting your cash a few weeks out and build a small reserve so a slow month doesn't become a crisis.
- **Tidy up how you work.** Smooth out your main day-to-day tasks to save time and cut waste. Simple tools or automation can help.
- **Keep customers coming back.** Repeat customers are cheaper than new ones. Focus on service and follow-up so people come back.

### Success — Comfortable & Steady

**Your main goal:** Keep things stable and running smoothly, and free up more of your own time.

- **Fine-tune what works.** Polish your existing systems for quality and efficiency. Look for small, steady improvements rather than big changes.
- **Think about the future.** If you might step back or sell one day, start gently planning — develop leaders or note what a future handover could look like.
- **Protect what you've built.** Review your finances, contracts, and insurance so an unexpected problem can't undo your hard work.
- **Step back a little.** Keep delegating so the business leans on you less and you get to enjoy the rewards of what you've created.

### Success — Ready to Grow

**Your main goal:** Get ready for controlled, well-planned growth.

- **Make a growth plan.** Decide where growth will come from — new customers, products, or markets — and set clear, measurable targets.
- **Build your team.** Hire and train managers who can take more off your plate, so you're free to focus on the bigger picture.
- **Line up the money.** Make sure you have the funding and financial systems to support growth before you push the accelerator.
- **Test before you leap.** Start small with a pilot in your new market or product line, learn from it, then scale what works.

### Growing Fast

**Your main goal:** Manage fast growth well and make the organization more professional.

- **Get organized.** Put clear roles, reporting lines, and team structures in place so a bigger company doesn't turn into chaos.
- **Stay ahead of cash.** Fast growth eats cash. Forecast carefully and manage it closely so a busy stretch doesn't cause a cash crunch.
- **Hire and keep good people.** Recruit actively, bring new people on board well, and build a culture that makes strong employees want to stay.
- **Use systems that scale.** Invest in tools and processes that can handle much higher volume without falling over.

### Large & Established

**Your main goal:** Stay a leader, keep innovating, and set up the business for the long haul.

- **Keep innovating.** Invest in new products, research, and technology so competitors don't pass you by.
- **Grow through partnerships.** Look at alliances, partnerships, or acquisitions to reach new customers and add new strengths.
- **Stay efficient.** Keep finding ways to run leaner and control costs — without cutting the quality or creativity that got you here.
- **Develop your people.** Invest in leaders, keep employees engaged, and plan ahead so the right people are ready for tomorrow's roles.
- **Spread your reach.** Explore new regions or customer groups so you're not too dependent on any single market.

## A quick note

This quiz gives general guidance based on a well-known small-business growth framework. Every business is different, so treat these results as a helpful starting point for your own planning — and a good conversation with a trusted advisor.

Based on the five stages of small-business growth by Neil C. Churchill and Virginia L. Lewis, published in the *Harvard Business Review* (1983): <https://hbr.org/1983/05/the-five-stages-of-small-business-growth>
